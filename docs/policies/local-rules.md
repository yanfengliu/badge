# Badge local policy

These repo-specific rules bind alongside the fleet constitution and make its boundaries concrete for Badge.

Each line below opens one rule. Its section links the file that holds the section's rules in full, with the reasons and evidence of each; open that file when the work reaches the rule. A new rule goes in full into its section's file, and its opening sentence onto that section's list here.

- [Personal data and Git](rules/personal-data-and-git.md):
  - Git tracks product code, schemas, migrations, curated catalogue source, computed-goal rules, Studio prompt templates, published renderer manifests, optional small pack registry records, tests, and deliberately promoted small source assets.
  - Never commit activations, dates, notes, sayings, visibility choices, user-created private definitions, Studio uploads, ordinary generated candidates, selected personal artwork, backups, provider credentials, account selectors, session details, or logs containing personal prompts or memories.
  - New definitions created in the archive and their issued authoring requests remain local and outside browse, plan, and activation flows until an explicitly imported Studio pack targets them with a published visual.
  - The sole Archive-to-Studio handoff is an explicitly previewed `.badgebrief` containing a random request ID, local definition ID, semantic revision, title, criterion, optional deliberately included description, schema version, and canonical digest.
  - Generated and uploaded Studio media is heavyweight local data by default.
- [Curated catalogue authoring](rules/curated-catalogue-authoring.md):
  - Git may track a deliberately promoted compact catalogue source study when it is a reviewed release-authoring input with stable identity, exact asset and prompt digests, bounded provenance, an explicit reason for promotion, and compliance with the fleet binary ceilings.
  - Each promoted generated source declares an accessible visual description, sanitized generation workflow and content origin, owner-directed rights basis, recorded exact-prompt association, and deterministic normalization and thumbnail recipes.
  - Catalogue result lists use the integrity-bound `128 × 128` thumbnail tier and reserve the full canonical source for one selected badge at a time — the same on-demand `896 × 896` study that supplies the activation texture; no intermediate preview tier ships.
  - Curated art styles and prompt recipes are versioned contracts.
  - Every curated catalogue expansion must review gaps across style family, medium, mood, subject, palette, and composition.
  - A later revision of an existing style ID, candidate role, or prompt recipe may be introduced only after its references and compiler dispatch carry the complete revision and keep the prior implementation callable.
  - Style language may use broad historical movements, media, and techniques as visual qualities, but it never asks a generator to imitate a named creator or copy a recognizable work, character, composition, logo, or trade dress.
  - Every generated catalogue candidate remains text-free, full-bleed source art.
  - A collection contact sheet proves coverage and broad cohesion, not candidate conformance.
  - A selected Studio source study becomes an activatable Archive badge only through the generated discovery catalogue pack fixture: `scripts/write-catalogue-pack-fixtures.mjs` reads the safe projection plus authoring records, verifies every canonical source hash against its on-disk bytes, binds one deterministic per-set render recipe and one record-private two-quotation bank from `@badge/catalogue-quotation-data`, and emits digest-bound `catalogue-pack-*.ts` modules under `@badge/catalogue-fixtures/catalogue-pack`.
- [Archive and Studio boundary](rules/archive-and-studio-boundary.md):
  - Badge serves one host document at `/`, where Collection, Timeline, and Discover are the primary sections selected through root-document view state; legacy HTML navigation under `/studio` redirects into the same document.
  - The archive never exposes art generation, prompt, candidate, provider, or publish controls, and its own screens render every badge read-only; the single `Adjust in Badge Studio` action on a badge's page routes into Studio rather than editing in place.
  - Badge Studio adjusts exactly eight things about one already-created badge: its picture, shape, material, border color, border width, tags, quote, and which collections it belongs to.
  - The Archive owns the adjustment.
  - The only thing that crosses between the two surfaces is `@badge/studio-adjustment-contract`: the Archive projects one badge — title, criterion, collected flag, current and catalogue image, catalogue recipe, the overlay's own values, tags, opaque collection keys and labels, and the badge's quotation bank — and Studio returns a submission.
  - The host hides the surface it is not showing, and a hidden surface keeps its state while running no effects.
  - The host hides the surface it is not showing, and a hidden surface runs no effects.
  - Badge Studio keeps one own-image action, and it receives exactly one piece of lifecycle information: whether the badge is already collected, which seals its face and quote.
  - Archive Discover consumes only the Git-tracked safe projection of deliberately promoted catalogue studies, their bounded eager list thumbnails, and the integrity-bound on-demand canonical source tier (`assets/discovery-sources.manifest.json`, 896-pixel studies at or below `256 KiB` each) that supplies activation textures and repair bytes, with the owner's adjustments laid over that projection so a badge appears on the shelf they moved it to and their own tags are searchable words.
- [Persistence and recovery](rules/persistence-and-recovery.md):
  - Persistence is schema-versioned, exportable, and migration-tested.
  - A `.badgearchive` container is byte-compared against the canonical encoding of the state it parses to, so every record field added with a schema default changes that encoding for every backup written before it existed and would refuse them all.
  - Local startup probes the unified Badge marker at `/` on every run.
  - Launcher lifecycle, browser, and terminal verification use branded task-owned state targets confined under ignored `tmp/local-startup/` paths and clean only their own records and listeners in a `finally` path.
  - Archive and Studio backups live outside the repository as distinct `.badgearchive` and `.badgestudio` formats.
  - Do not delete an original, selected asset, or backup merely because the current screen no longer references it.
- [Product integrity](rules/product-integrity.md):
  - The initial product is single-user and local-first.
  - Achievement activation rests on personal honesty.
  - Every newly created badge begins with one preselected source-checked quotation attributed to a real historical figure.
  - The saying remains independent from the art, but it is no longer freeform authoring.
  - Live quotation regeneration discloses the provider and exact outbound fields before badge text leaves the device.
  - A live provider call occurs only after the final `Regenerate with Claude` approval or a later `Regenerate quote` action, and each action starts at most one attempt without automatic retries or fan-out; creation, page load, selection, activation, ceremony, reload, restore, and background work never call a saying provider.
  - Prompt v3 and quotation contract v6 permit only the closed response `{ quotationId }` selecting one exact ID from the disclosed shortlist.
  - The canonical saying request is capped at `12 KiB` within a `16 KiB` same-origin route body.
  - Badge Studio keeps generated artwork text-free.
  - Shape, material, border color, border width, crop, and positioning remain editable structured properties in Badge Studio rather than being baked irreversibly into the only copy of the art.
  - The badge itself is a real-time 3D artifact in Badge Studio and archive detail views.
  - Mouse rotation, bounded zoom, responsive lighting, a separate light-adjustment control, keyboard equivalents, reset, reduced-motion behavior, and a clear GPU fallback are product requirements rather than optional polish.
  - Interactive inspection on touch devices must arbitrate intent before pointer capture: vertical movement remains page-owned, horizontal movement may claim badge manipulation, and release returns the viewer to an unengaged state.
  - Those examination controls remain available in Archive detail, Timeline inspection, Badge Studio, and the first activation ceremony.
  - Activation data commits before its ceremony begins.
- [Visual quality](rules/visual-quality.md):
  - The selected product language is a quiet premium field archive with substantial crafted objects, exact typography, generous space, restrained color, and clean motion.
  - When an Archive or Studio surface's visual direction is accepted or materially changed, update the canonical implemented-surface record in `docs/design/visual-direction.md` in the same session and promote a bounded fixture-only reference capture that names its route, viewport, state, and date.
  - Avoid cartoon rendering, careless generated art, souvenir-shop kitsch, cheap gradients, glassmorphism, confetti, loot effects, excessive glow, fake rarity, cluttered dashboards, and typography embedded in generated images.
  - The owner's standing Moebius preference maps to the creator-neutral `luminous-ligne-claire` source-art direction.
  - Do not fake the required object with CSS perspective on a flat image or bake highlights and shadows into the only authoritative preview.
  - Archive and Badge Studio remain desktop-first in composition but are complete phone-capable applications.
  - Visual changes are not complete until the relevant full flow is exercised in a real browser and inspected at wide and compact desktop, phone portrait down to the `320px` floor, and short landscape, including loading, empty, error, keyboard, touch-scroll ownership, reduced-motion, and GPU-fallback states when applicable.
