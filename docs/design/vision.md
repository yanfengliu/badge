# Vision

## What this is

Badge is a personal archive of meaningful achievements that helps one person feel proud of what they have done, remember the story around it, and stay curious about what to explore next.

It combines the collectibility of national-park stamps and physical badges, the structure of an achievement system, and the intimacy of a private memory collection.

There is no prize, authority, or external proof. Earning rests on personal honesty.

## Product pillars

1. **Pride and memory come first.** Opening the app should bring back the feeling of visiting a place, finishing a book, earning a degree, or completing another meaningful chapter.
2. **Achievements feel made, not counted.** Each badge is a substantial interactive 3D object with beautiful art, credible material and lighting, a concise saying, and enough context to preserve the memory.
3. **Goals invite exploration.** Suggested and planned badges show what could come next without streak pressure, points, punishment, or competitive ranking.
4. **The collection can grow broadly.** Travel, reading, education, craft, health, relationships, and personal milestones use the same small set of primitives instead of unrelated feature systems.
5. **The owner keeps the archive.** The first version is private and local-first, with durable disk backup and a strict separation between Git-tracked system definitions and local personal state.

## Canonical examples

- **Visited Yosemite National Park** exercises a prebuilt travel catalogue, an occurrence date range, a location-specific artifact, and progress toward a larger collection.
- **Read _Sapiens_** exercises a custom or suggested cultural achievement and a concise reflective memory.
- **Finished a bachelor's degree** exercises a long time range and a singular life milestone.
- **Visited every U.S. national park** exercises a computed achievement derived from individual park activations.

Keep these as early fixtures because together they test the breadth the product promises.

## Core loop

Discover or create an achievement → shape its visual identity → keep it as a plan or activate it honestly → enjoy a crisp reveal → revisit the badge and its memory → see meaningful progress toward larger collections.

## Scope stance

Version one is a desktop-first local web application for one person.

It supports prebuilt and custom collections, suggested and handmade badges, planned and earned states, multiple art candidates, uploaded and reprocessed images, appearance customization, mouse-driven 3D examination with dynamic lighting, activation memories, automatic composite achievements, and local backup and restore.

The U.S. National Parks collection ships as a versioned catalogue rather than requiring every park to be entered by hand.

Accounts, authentication, cloud sync, public publishing, viewing other users, social reactions, competitive mechanics, and external achievement verification are deferred.

Visibility inheritance is modeled now so later presentation and sharing do not require reshaping personal data, but its public behavior is deliberately not defined yet.

## Emotional and ethical stance

The product encourages without judging.

An unearned badge is an invitation, not a failure; an earned badge is a memory, not a claim to status over someone else.

The system may suggest what to try and generate art or a clever saying, but only the user decides what belongs in their life and when it was earned.
