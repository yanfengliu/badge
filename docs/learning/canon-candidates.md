## An aggregate view proves coverage, not conformance: a contact sheet, grid, montage, or proof sheet answers "is there one of each" and never "is each one right", and it answers the first just as confidently when the second answer is no — inspect each item at the artifact's own native resolution before promoting it.

**From:** badge / "A contact sheet proves collection coverage, not source conformance; inspect every promoted generated source at native resolution against factual cues, explicit exclusions, and all four edges."

**Why it has no gate:** the judgement it demands — does this image depict the thing its brief names — is the part no assertion replaces; that is why the rule is "go and look". Badge gates the _record_ that the looking happened against specific bytes (`native-source-review.test.ts`), but the looking itself has no mechanical trigger, and the claim generalises past images to any batch of generated artifacts.

**What it adds to what the constitution already says:** the canon already has "Verify visual work visually … One framing is not a check: sweep several camera angles and zoom levels." That addresses one item seen too narrowly. This is the opposite failure: _many_ items seen together, which reads as thorough precisely because it is broad. A sweep of angles on a grid of thumbnails is still a grid of thumbnails. The missing words are **aggregate view** and **the artifact's native resolution** — distinct from camera zoom, because the defect is in the asset's own pixels, not in how the camera framed it.

**Anchor:** 2026-08-23. A labelled 63-image national-park contact sheet looked broad and cohesive; an independent pass at native `896 × 896` found inset work boundaries, the wrong waterfall geometry, an oak-like crown on General Sherman, geyser-like Hot Springs water, fantasy Mammoth Cave forms, and incorrect Guadalupe Mountains and Katmai silhouettes. Ten park sources were rejected and regenerated.

**Recurrence that proves the prose was not enough:** on 2026-08-27, four days after the lesson was written, all 347 authoring projects were rerendered code-native and the visual pass that followed inspected **exact-48px proof sheets** — an aggregate view at the smallest scale in the catalogue. Every promoted national-park source shipped from that rerender; none of them was the byte reviewed on 2026-08-23. The lesson was in `lessons.md` the whole time.

## A review is a fact about specific bytes, not about a collection: bind it to the digest of what was inspected, so regenerating the artifact strands its review instead of inheriting it.

**From:** badge / the same lesson, generalised from the gate built for it.

**Why it has no gate here beyond this repo:** it is a design rule for how any repo records human or model judgement about generated artifacts; each repo needs its own binding, and there is no fleet-level artifact to assert against.

**Why it matters:** "reviewed" attaches to a catalogue in prose and silently survives every regeneration of that catalogue's contents. Attached to a digest it cannot: new bytes have no review, and the gate says so by name. This is what converts "we reviewed the parks" — still true, still useless — into "these 63 digests were looked at, and two of them are no longer what ships."

**Anchor:** 2026-08-23 audit vs. the 2026-08-27 rerender, above. Sequoia's anchored digest `f3743d4319406e094f04d45ec0dfa74f5ebc520721a38c6145e7f19655356f47` is not the promoted digest; `875acb498abf323283ed431060e7bc39fd3bca9dc9c2dff6475f91ae9a17ed74` is. Measured 2026-09-02.
